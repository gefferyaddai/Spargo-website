The problem

Why Calgary grocery prices vary so much.

Grocery prices aren't set city-wide. Each chain, discount grocer and independent store prices its own shelves, and those prices move every week as flyers change and sales rotate. A store that's cheapest for produce can be expensive for dairy or pantry staples.

Package sizes make it harder. One store sells a 1.5 kg bag, another a 2 kg bag, and a third runs a two-for deal. Without doing the maths, it's hard to tell which is actually the better price.

And Calgary is spread out. Stores are scattered across the NW, NE, SW and SE, and a cheaper basket across the city isn't much of a saving once you count the drive. The cheapest store is only useful if it's also reasonably close to you.

Do it yourself

How to compare grocery prices in Calgary today.

You don't need an app to start saving. These habits work at any store.

Compare the unit price, not the sticker price

Most shelf tags show a price per 100 g, per kg or per litre. Comparing that number is the fastest way to see which size or brand is really cheaper, especially when a "big pack" isn't the bargain it looks like.

Check more than one store's weekly flyer

Most chains publish a new flyer every week. Comparing two or three flyers for the items you buy most often shows you quickly which store is running the better deals that week.

Ask about price matching

Some retailers in Canada will match a competitor's advertised price. Policies differ by store and change over time, so check the current policy at customer service before you rely on it.

Know what you'll pay at the till

Alberta has no provincial sales tax, and most basic groceries are zero-rated for GST, so for staples the shelf price is usually what you pay. Snacks, soft drinks, candy and many prepared foods do carry the 5% GST, so a basket heavy on those will total more than the shelf prices suggest.

Weigh the drive

A second stop is worth it only if the saving beats the extra time and fuel. Planning the stop around a trip you're already making usually tips the balance.
